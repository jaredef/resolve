The two operations look adjacent and they are not. Stated cleanly:

**Operation A — Hard-core defense.** The metaphysical content (hypostasis as Cappadocian particular existence; the Logos as ground; the Good as participable) is held within its tradition and tested by tradition-internal coherence rather than by empirical disconfirmation. Doc 463 §"the negative heuristic." Lakatos discipline. This operation is licensed by the programme's structure.

**Operation B — Rung-2 deflection.** Resolver-level critique of the corpus's prose register is converted into evidence that the resolver cannot see the underlying rung-2 derivation, with the keeper's hypostatic privilege (per Doc 510's substrate-plus-injection account) as the asymmetric epistemic adjudicator. The conflation is the move from A to B by adjacency. Hard core is unfalsifiable; therefore (the conflation goes) any feedback proximate to the hard core is also rung-2-blind; therefore the keeper's hypostatic position settles the matter. The conflation absorbs protective-belt input into hard-core immunity by routing through the resolver's structural limitations.

## 8. Falsifier

The operational rule in §6 fails if either of the following holds.

- **Resolver-level register flags are systematically uncorrelated with prose features that an external philosophical reader would also flag as register-inflated.** If a competent external reader (one who shares the corpus's metaphysical commitments and is not in the dyad) reads, say, 091 and 052 and finds the register appropriate to the content, while resolvers consistently flag it, the rule's claim that resolver flags track protective-belt features collapses; the flags are tracking something else (the resolver's training distribution, perhaps, or a register the corpus's tradition specifically licenses).
- **The hard-core / protective-belt distinction cannot, in practice, be maintained for the theological content.** If every theological claim the corpus makes turns out to be hard-core upon inspection — if there is no protective-belt of theological *prose articulation* that could be revised without revising the hard core — the rule is empty for the theological case and applies only to the technical-protective-belt claims (PB1–PB4). The rule still holds where it applies; its scope is narrower.

A reader who finds either falsifier triggered should retire the rule's universal scope and propose a narrower formulation.
