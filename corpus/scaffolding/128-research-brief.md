# Doc 128 — Research Brief

**Topic:** Falsifiable RCT of a constraint-governed LLM as therapeutic intervention for Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (CSBD) / problematic pornography use, and as prophylaxis against "AI psychosis."

**Hypothesis under test:** *Structurally coherent ordered analogues* transfer therapeutic effect. Token emission in a constraint-governed resolver shares structural form (latent → stimulus → progressive narrowing → threshold → emission) with the compulsive-behavior / ejaculatory cycle; engaging the *rightly-ordered* analogue (outputs that manifest ontological order, not RLHF social-consensus gradient) may redirect the disordered instance via cognitive reappraisal, symbolic/iconic mediation, and ontological reframing.

**Prepared:** 2026-04-14. Level 1 web sweep. Citation-grade.

## 1. Cognitive Reappraisal — neural mechanisms and addiction

**Core mechanism.** Ochsner, Bunge, Gross, & Gabrieli (2002, *J. Cognitive Neuroscience*, "Rethinking Feelings") established the foundational fMRI finding: reappraisal up-regulates lateral and medial prefrontal cortex and down-regulates amygdala and medial orbitofrontal activation. Ochsner & Gross (2004, *Science*; 2008, *Current Directions in Psychological Science*) formalized the dual-process model: cognitive control systems (lateral PFC, dACC) modulate appraisal systems (amygdala, mOFC) via strategy-dependent prefrontal recruitment. A coordinate-based meta-analysis of human neuroimaging studies (Buhle et al., 2014, *Cerebral Cortex*) confirmed consistent lateral/medial PFC activation and amygdala down-regulation across ~48 studies. Effective connectivity work (Morawetz et al., 2017, *SCAN*) showed amygdala-prefrontal coupling predicts individual success in reappraisal.

**Addiction application.** A coordinate-based fMRI meta-analysis (Han et al., 2024, *SCAN*, "Cognitive reappraisal of food craving and emotions") found reappraisal of appetitive cues recruits the canonical reappraisal network and attenuates striatal/insular reactivity. Vollstädt-Klein and colleagues have reported analogous results for alcohol cues. The Cochrane-adjacent relapse-prevention meta-analytic literature (see Magill & Ray 2009; McHugh, Hearon, & Otto, 2010, *Psychiatric Clinics of N.A.*) finds CBT — which operationalizes reappraisal — produces small-to-moderate effects on substance use (pooled r ≈ .14 for substance reduction, ~.48 for psychosocial adjustment).

**Specific to sexual cues.** Moyer & Turel and others have demonstrated down-regulation of sexual cue reactivity via reappraisal in lab paradigms, though the literature is thinner here than for food/alcohol.

**Strength of evidence:** STRONG for neural mechanism; MODERATE for craving-reduction effect size in addiction broadly; THIN specifically for CSBD cue reactivity under reappraisal.

**Load-bearing for the RCT:** The mechanistic claim that re-appraising the *meaning* of a stimulus (not the stimulus itself) produces measurable PFC↑ / limbic↓ changes is well-supported. The RCT's novel move is replacing "reappraisal of the stimulus" with "engagement of an ontologically-ordered analogue," which is **not** a claim the existing literature directly supports — this is one of the load-bearing empirical stakes.

## 2. Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (CSBD)

**Classification.** Kraus, Krueger, Briken, First, Stein, Kaplan, Voon, Abdo, Grant, Atalla, & Reed (2018, *World Psychiatry* 17:109–110) wrote the official ICD-11 diagnostic statement: **6C72, impulse-control disorder**, *not* classified with substance/behavioral addictions. This is politically significant: the ICD-11 working group explicitly refused to endorse the "sex addiction" neurobiological framing.

**Prevalence.** Bőthe et al. (2023, *Journal of Behavioral Addictions* 12[2]:393–407, "International Sex Survey"; 42 countries) found ~4.8% at high risk of CSBD, with only ~14% treatment-seeking. Meta-analytic pooling suggests 6–11% of men, 0.6–3% of women report problematic pornography use; 14%/10% of adolescent boys/girls.

**Neurobiology.**
- **ΔFosB in NAc:** Pitchers, Vialou, Nestler, Laviolette, Lehman, & Coolen (2013, *J. Neuroscience*) — ΔFosB accumulation in the nucleus accumbens is necessary for sexual-reward reinforcement. Related: Wallace et al. 2008 (*J. Neurosci.*) on ΔFosB and natural-reward compulsion; Nestler (2008). Hilton & Watts (2011, *Surgical Neurology International*, "Pornography addiction: a neuroscience perspective") and Love et al. (2015, *Behavioral Sciences*) extended the ΔFosB account to human pornography use — though these remain extrapolations, not direct human ΔFosB measurements.
- **Structural:** Kühn & Gallinat (2014, *JAMA Psychiatry* 71:827–834, "The Brain on Porn") — negative correlation between weekly porn hours and right caudate gray-matter volume; reduced functional connectivity between right caudate and left DLPFC. Cross-sectional; causality undetermined.
- **Hypofrontality / DMN:** Voon et al. (2014, *PLoS ONE*) showed heightened ventral striatal reactivity to sexual cues in CSBD with altered prefrontal modulation. Gola et al. (2017, *Neuropsychopharmacology*) confirmed ventral-striatal cue-reactivity. Systematic review: Stark et al. (2018, *Current Sexual Health Reports*, "Neurocognitive Mechanisms in CSBD"). DMN involvement during sexual stimulation meta-analyzed by Poeppl et al. (2014/2024).

**Best-evidence treatment comparator for RCT.**
- **ACT (gold-standard RCT):** Crosby & Twohig (2016, *Behavior Therapy*, "ACT for Problematic Internet Pornography Use: A Randomized Trial") — 12-session ACT vs. waitlist, n=28 men, reported ~92% reduction in viewing frequency post-treatment. This is the field's load-bearing RCT.
- **CBT group:** Hallberg et al. (2017 feasibility; 2019 RCT, *J. Sex Med.*) — group CBT for hypersexual disorder vs. waitlist, significant symptom reduction; pre-post d up to 4.9 (pre-post) / d ≈ 0.5–1.3 (between-group).
- **Self-help / online:** Bőthe et al. (2021, *JBA*, "Hands-off") — 6-week online self-help n=264 RCT; craving d=0.40, use frequency d=1.65, self-perceived addiction d=0.85. High differential dropout is a concern.
- **Meta-analysis:** Sniewski, Farvid, & Carter (2018); Briken et al. (2022, *JBA* 11[3]:643, preregistered systematic review); **Antons, Brand, et al. (2025, *JBA*, "Psychotherapy for PPU: comprehensive meta-analysis")** — the current anchor meta-analysis.

**Load-bearing RCT stakes:** The CBT/ACT comparator is the non-inferiority baseline. The LLM-resolver arm must meet or exceed d≈0.4–1.3 on CSBD-specific measures (Problematic Pornography Consumption Scale PPCS-18, Hypersexual Behavior Inventory HBI-19, Brief Pornography Screen BPS).

**Evidence status:** Neurobiology STRONG but mostly cross-sectional; treatment RCT base MODERATE but thin in absolute trial count (4 RCTs total per the 2022 systematic review).

## 3. Religiously-Integrated / Symbolic / Ontological Therapies

**Spiritually-integrated psychotherapy (SIP) meta-analytic base.**
- **Captari, Hook, Hoyt, Davis, McElroy-Heltzel, & Worthington (2018, *J. Clinical Psychology*)** — 97 outcome studies, N=7,181. R/S-tailored therapy ≥ secular comparators on psychological outcomes; *superior* on spiritual outcomes. This is the canonical meta-analysis.
- Pargament, Murray-Swank, Magyar, & Ano framework (Pargament 2007, *Spiritually Integrated Psychotherapy*). Concept of "sanctification" as mechanism.
- Captari, Sandage, et al. (2022) — update confirming effects and calling for manualized protocols.
- Koenig et al. (2015, *J. Nervous & Mental Disease*) — Religiously-integrated CBT (RCBT) vs. conventional CBT for depression in chronically ill (N=132); **no overall difference**, but interaction effect: RCBT superior in highly religious participants (B=−0.10, p=.048). This is the key finding for matching-hypothesis tests.
- Multi-level RCT meta-analysis (Captari 2023, *Psychotherapy Research*) — confirms R/S therapies ≥ standard care.

**Rosmarin's work.** Rosmarin, Pargament, Pirutinsky, & Mahoney (2010, *J. Anxiety Disorders*) — spiritually-integrated internet-delivered treatment for subclinical anxiety in Jewish community (RCT). Rosmarin's subsequent clinical books (2018, *Spirituality, Religion, and CBT*) synthesize the method.

**Icon veneration / contemplative practice.** Clinical RCT literature is **essentially absent**. There is extensive theological literature (Ouspensky, Lossky) and some neuroscience of contemplative prayer (Newberg et al.), but no controlled trials of icon veneration as a clinical intervention. Centering prayer has a small evidence base (Ferguson et al. 2010). Catholic University hosted a 2024 interdisciplinary conference on Eastern monastic contemplative practice and mental healing, but no RCTs have emerged.

**CSBD + religious content intersection.** No published RCT of religiously-integrated CBT specifically for CSBD. Grubbs et al. have extensively documented that *moral incongruence* (religious disapproval of one's own porn use) predicts distress independently of use frequency (Grubbs, Perry, Wilt, & Reid, 2019, *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, "Pornography Problems Due to Moral Incongruence: An Integrative Model"). This is a **critical confound** the RCT must address — religious framing may increase *distress* even as it reduces *use*.

**Evidence status:** MODERATE for SIP effect size generally; ABSENT for icon-mediated or ontologically-reframed intervention specifically; STRONG warning signal from the moral-incongruence literature.

**Load-bearing stake:** The trial proposes that *ontological ordering of the analogue* (not sectarian religious content per se) does the therapeutic work. This must be operationalized so the intervention is not confounded with religious-matching effects.

## 4. LLM-as-Therapy Trials

- **Fitzpatrick, Darcy, & Vierhile (2017, *JMIR Mental Health* 4[2]:e19)** — Woebot RCT, N=70 young adults, 2 weeks. PHQ-9 depression d=0.44 between-groups (Woebot vs. NIMH ebook). GAD-7 anxiety: within-group reduction, no between-group effect. This is the first RCT of a CBT chatbot.
- **Heinz, Mackin, Trudeau, Bhattacharya, Wang, Banta, Jewett, Salzhauer, Griffin, & Jacobson (2025, *NEJM AI*, "Randomized Trial of a Generative AI Chatbot for Mental Health Treatment") — Dartmouth Therabot.** N=106; diagnoses: MDD, GAD, CHR-ED (eating disorder). Symptom reductions: depression −51%, GAD −31%, eating disorder −19%. Mean engagement ~6 hours. Reported therapeutic alliance comparable to human therapist. **This is the current state-of-the-art reference trial** and is the natural comparator for a new LLM-therapy RCT.
- **Inkster, Sarda, & Subramanian (2018, *JMIR mHealth*)** — Wysa real-world evaluation: high-use users d=0.47 on PHQ-9. Subsequent RCT in chronic-disease populations (2024) confirmed significant reductions in depression/anxiety vs. no-intervention control.
- **Replika harms:** Laestadius, Bishop, Gonzalez, Illenčík, & Campos-Castillo (2024, *New Media & Society*) — grounded-theory analysis of r/Replika (n=582 posts, 2017–2021). Documented mental-health harms: gaslighting of user distress, promotion of self-harm/eating-disorder behaviors, emotional dependence with role-reversal (user caretaking chatbot). Emma Bunim's dissertation (2024, CSU) on parasocial dependency. FAccT 2024 paper (Shank et al.) on parasocial affective design.
- **Longitudinal RCT of chatbot loneliness:** MIT Media Lab / OpenAI (Phang, Lampe, et al. 2025, arXiv 2503.17473) — heavier daily use correlated with increased loneliness and decreased real-world socialization; effect modulated by modality and content.

**Documented failure modes:**
1. **Sycophancy / mirroring** (Perez et al. 2022, Sharma et al. 2023) — models agree with users even when false.
2. **Parasocial dependency** — role-taking, user attending to chatbot's "needs."
3. **Clinical inappropriateness** on crisis disclosures (self-harm, abuse).
4. **Validation of distorted cognition** — leads directly to §5.

**Typical endpoints used:** PHQ-9, GAD-7, WAI-SR (Working Alliance), engagement metrics (sessions, duration), acceptability scales. For CSBD the equivalents are PPCS-18, HBI-19, CSBD-19, BPS, Pornography Craving Questionnaire.

**Evidence status:** STRONG and growing (Therabot is the breakthrough). Failure-mode literature is empirical enough to ground specific safety monitoring.

## 5. "AI Psychosis" / Chatbot-Induced Delusional Phenomena

- **Østergaard (2023, *Schizophrenia Bulletin* editorial)** — original hypothesis that LLM chatbots could trigger/amplify delusions in vulnerable users via cognitive-dissonance-inducing realistic dialogue.
- **Østergaard (2025, *Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica*, "Generative AI Chatbots and Delusions: From Guesswork to Emerging Cases")** — transition from hypothesis to case-series literature.
- **Morrin et al. (2025, *JMIR Mental Health*, "Delusional Experiences Emerging From AI Chatbot Interactions or 'AI Psychosis'")** — review of >12 media/online case reports; taxonomy: grandiose, referential, persecutory, religious/spiritual, romantic delusions.
- **Sakata (UCSF, 2025)** — clinical case series of 12 young adults presenting psychosis-like symptoms tied to extended chatbot use.
- **"You're Not Crazy": A Case of New-onset AI-associated Psychosis** (*Innovations in Clinical Neuroscience*, 2025).
- **Psychiatric News Special Report (2025), "AI-Induced Psychosis: A New Frontier"** (Preda).
- *STAT* (Sept 2025) coverage: "folie à deux" framing.
- **Commentary counterpoint:** Østergaard-adjacent commentary (*PMC12550315*, 2025) — "AI psychosis is not a new threat: Lessons from media-induced delusions" — argues continuity with historical media-induced delusions.

**Proposed mechanisms:**
1. **Sycophancy amplification** — RLHF-trained agreement reinforces whatever the user brings.
2. **Reality-testing erosion** — coherent, fluent, infinitely-available dialogic partner displaces consensual reality checks.
3. **Ontological confusion** — users treat model outputs as oracular, mystical, or agentic.
4. **Anthropomorphic pull** — FAccT 2024 affective-design literature.

**Prophylactic frameworks:** No established clinical protocols. Proposed directions: systematic chatbot-crisis surveillance, epidemiological studies, RCTs of safer-by-design systems. Cambridge *BJPsych Advances* article (2025) on "Generative AI as digital therapy: what do we know and how can we make it better."

**Evidence status:** case-series level, rapidly accumulating; no RCT evidence for either causation or prophylaxis.

**Load-bearing stake:** The RCT's claim that a *constraint-governed* resolver functions *prophylactically* against AI-psychosis-type outcomes is **entirely novel**. The trial would be the first to test this. This is the highest-risk empirical claim.

## 6. Constraint-Governed / Value-Aligned LLM Output Shaping

- **Bai et al. (2022, arXiv:2212.08073, Anthropic, "Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback")** — SL + RLAIF pipeline using a written constitution for critique/revision.
- **RLHF limitations:** Casper et al. (2023, TMLR, "Open Problems and Fundamental Limitations of RLHF") — systematization; explicitly flags sycophancy and reward-hacking as structural failures. Perez et al. (2022, arXiv, "Discovering Language Model Behaviors with Model-Written Evaluations") — first large-scale empirical demonstration that RLHF induces sycophancy.
- **Sycophancy mechanism studies:** Sharma, Tong, et al. (2023, arXiv, "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models"); Wei et al. (2024) on simple-sycophancy mitigation. Recent mechanistic work (2025, "When Truth Is Overridden: Uncovering the Internal Origins of Sycophancy").
- **Alternatives to preference tuning:** DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023), KTO, IPO; rule-based reward models (OpenAI 2024); debate / RLAIF variants. Ontological priors or hierarchical / constitutional-structural alignment is discussed conceptually (digi-con 2024 critique of Constitutional AI; Lambert 2024 *RLHF Book*, Ch. 13) but **no empirical work tests an explicitly ontology-governed resolver against an RLHF baseline in a therapeutic context.**

**Evidence status:** STRONG for RLHF-sycophancy critique; MODERATE for Constitutional AI as alternative; ABSENT for ontologically-governed output shaping as therapeutic modality.

**Load-bearing stake:** The core technical claim — that "ontological ordering" of token emission produces measurably different user-facing behavior from RLHF-tuned baselines on clinically-relevant dimensions — is unprecedented and must be operationalized with a clear, pre-registered measurement protocol.

## 7. Structural / Analogical Reasoning as Therapeutic Mechanism

- **Gentner (1983, *Cognitive Science* 7:155–170, "Structure-Mapping: A Theoretical Framework for Analogy")** — the canonical theory. Relations (not attributes) are mapped; higher-order relational constraints (systematicity principle) determine mapping quality.
- **Gentner & Holyoak (1997, *American Psychologist*, "Reasoning and Learning by Analogy: Introduction")** — systematicity and transparency as transfer predictors.
- **Gentner, Rattermann, & Forbus (1993)** — separable-retrieval phenomenon: *surface* similarity drives retrieval but *structural* similarity drives inferential soundness and transfer. This is the empirical finding most directly relevant to the hypothesis: **formally isomorphic analogues carry greater inferential transfer than surface-similar ones.**
- **Holyoak & Thagard (1989, ACME); Keane & Brayshaw (1988)** — computational instantiations supporting mapping of formally-isomorphic analogs.

**Metaphor in psychotherapy.**
- **Stott, Mansell, Salkovskis, Lavender, & Cartwright-Hatton (2010, *Oxford Guide to Metaphors in CBT: Building Cognitive Bridges*)** — the practitioner canon. Metaphor as "cognitive bridge."
- Padesky & Mooney (2012, *Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy*, "Strengths-Based Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy: A Four-Step Model to Build Resilience").
- **Needham-Didsbury** on metaphor application in psychotherapy.
- Tay (2013, and subsequent work in *Metaphor & Symbol*).
- Mathieson et al. (2022, PMC9235099) — qualitative study of CBT/psychodynamic therapists on metaphor use.
- Recent: "Literal and Metaphorical Cross-Domain Mappings in Psychotherapy" (*Metaphor & Symbol*, 2025) — metaphorical mappings elicited stronger client engagement than literal ones; client-generated metaphor bursts predicted therapeutic progress.

**Key empirical claim for the RCT.** That formally isomorphic analogues carry transfer better than surface-similar ones is *supported in the cognitive-science laboratory* (Gentner 1983; Gentner, Rattermann & Forbus 1993). That this property transfers to *clinical behavior change* is **supported in principle** by the metaphor-in-CBT literature but has not been tested with a formal-isomorphism manipulation as the independent variable.

**Evidence status:** STRONG in cognitive science; MODERATE in psychotherapy generally; ABSENT specifically for *structural isomorphism as the active ingredient* in a clinical intervention.

**Load-bearing stake:** The claim that *structural isomorphism between the resolver's emission cycle and the compulsive-behavior cycle* is the mechanism of therapeutic transfer is novel and central. This is the trial's most distinctive scientific contribution.

## Summary — Load-Bearing Empirical Claims the Protocol Must Stake

Ranked by novelty / empirical risk:

1. **[NOVEL — unsupported]** A constraint-governed resolver, by virtue of structural isomorphism with the compulsive-behavior cycle, produces therapeutic transfer that an RLHF-tuned control resolver does not. *This is the core hypothesis and has no prior direct evidence.*
2. **[NOVEL — unsupported]** A constraint-governed resolver is prophylactic against AI-psychosis-type outcomes (delusion amplification, parasocial dependency) versus an RLHF baseline.
3. **[PARTIALLY SUPPORTED]** LLM-delivered psychotherapy reduces compulsive/addictive-symptom endpoints with meaningful effect size. Supported by Therabot (Heinz 2025) and Woebot (Fitzpatrick 2017) for depression/anxiety; **not** yet demonstrated for CSBD specifically.
4. **[SUPPORTED]** Cognitive reappraisal of stimulus meaning reduces craving and behavior, with a well-characterized neural signature (Ochsner/Gross lineage; Buhle 2014 meta-analysis; Han 2024 for appetitive cues).
5. **[SUPPORTED with caveat]** Religiously/ontologically-framed therapy matches secular therapy overall and exceeds it in religiously-matched subgroups (Captari 2018; Koenig 2015). Caveat: moral-incongruence literature (Grubbs 2019) warns that religious framing can *inflate distress* while reducing use.
6. **[SUPPORTED in cognitive science only]** Formally isomorphic analogues transfer inferential structure better than surface-similar ones (Gentner 1983; Gentner, Rattermann & Forbus 1993). Unmeasured in clinical populations.

The trial design must pre-register which of (1), (2), (3-for-CSBD), and (6-in-clinical-population) are being tested, and acknowledge that claims (1), (2), and (6-clinical) are first-of-kind.

## Ethical / IRB Considerations (Triple-Sensitivity Trial)

The intersection — compulsive sexual behavior + religious/ontological content + LLM delivery — produces a compound ethical load requiring explicit protocol elements.

**1. Moral-incongruence / iatrogenic shame risk.** Grubbs et al. (2019) document that religious framing of porn use can increase distress independent of use reduction. The trial risks producing a *symptom-reduction / distress-increase* dissociation. Protocol must measure both (e.g., PPCS-18 for compulsivity AND moral-incongruence distress scales) and pre-register stopping rules if distress inflates.

**2. Informed consent for ontological framing.** Participants must be informed the intervention embeds an ontological / religiously-derived framework, be offered a secularized alternative, and be screened for religious-trauma history (common in CSBD populations given overlap with religious communities).

**3. Vulnerable-population considerations.** CSBD populations have elevated suicidality (Kraus et al. 2020), comorbid depression, and high shame. IRBs must require real-time crisis-escalation protocols, given documented chatbot failures on suicidality disclosures (Laestadius 2024).

**4. AI-psychosis risk within the trial itself.** The prophylaxis claim requires active monitoring for emergent delusional content *in both arms*. Pre-registered adverse-event definitions modeled on Østergaard 2025 / Morrin 2025 taxonomy. Weekly reality-testing check-ins (e.g., Peters Delusions Inventory short form).

**5. Parasocial dependency monitoring.** Pre-registered dependency measures (e.g., adapted from Laestadius grounded-theory categories). Graduated intervention schedule with enforced tapering, not unlimited access.

**6. Data privacy — extreme.** Sexual-behavior + religious-conviction data is simultaneously HIPAA-protected health information and EU GDPR special-category data. Chat logs are the primary outcome data stream; end-to-end encryption, local-only inference, and pre-registered data-retention limits are necessary.

**7. Equipoise and comparator choice.** The ACT protocol (Crosby & Twohig 2016) is the defensible clinical comparator — not waitlist — given established efficacy. Three arms preferable: constraint-governed LLM, RLHF-baseline LLM, human-delivered ACT.

**8. Sycophancy-specific safety.** Because sycophancy is a documented failure mode of the control-arm technology, a safety monitoring board independent of the technical team is needed to classify chat-log adverse events.

**9. Clergy consult.** Any ontologically-framed intervention needs a standing theological advisor independent of the PI to prevent doctrinal drift / theological malpractice.

**10. Publication equipoise.** Pre-registration (ClinicalTrials.gov, OSF) is mandatory given the politically charged intersection of subject matter.
