## II. Source Read

SEBoK distributes schedule management across surrounding pages. *Project Planning* names work-product sizing and scheduling, technical-review timing, technical cost and schedule estimation, and lead-time consideration. *Project Assessment and Control* names schedule monitoring against plans and corrective action. *The Nature of Project Management* and *PMBOK Guide* overview place schedule management as one of PMBOK's knowledge areas with formal techniques (Critical Path Method, PERT, resource leveling, schedule baselines, Earned Value). *Systems Engineering and Project Management* (SE-037) names schedule as a domain of joint PM-SE responsibility. Standards: ISO/IEC/IEEE 16326, PMBOK Guide 7e, INCOSE SE Handbook. Common artifacts: schedule baseline, critical path, milestone chart, earned-value report.

## III. Structural Read

**Form III — Multi-Keeper Composition (Doc 604), at schedule rung.** Schedule management is canonical Doc 604 territory. PM owns the integrated schedule baseline; SE owns the technical sequencing of design / verification / integration / validation activities; functional managers own resource availability. The reconciliation rung is the integrated schedule with formal change board. Doc 604 composition rule: coordination-by-rung (PM as coordinator over SE and functional inputs); negotiation-by-priority active under contention. Seventh Cluster B instance.

**Form IV — Pin-Art Model (Doc 270), with Doc 572 Appendix C temporal-concurrency.** A schedule is pin-art whose substrate is the realized temporal flow of work. Pin-sets are tasks, milestones, dependencies, resource constraints. The substrate passes through; the shape that emerges is the actual sequence of work, which is then re-pulverized against the baseline (SE-035 forward-pulverization at risk-rung). Doc 572 Appendix C reads schedule as the temporal-concurrency lattice's empirical instrument: every life-cycle rung is concurrently scheduled, with the schedule encoding the rung-coupling pattern.

**Form X — Institutional Ground (Doc 571), §X.5.** The schedule baseline as artifact lives at the organization-component (formal commitment, contract-binding); the day-to-day schedule discipline (status reporting, replanning, dependency-tracking) lives at the enterprise-component (accumulated working tradition). §X.5 explains the common pathology: a baseline that remains formally authoritative while the working practice has already replanned without updating it. Authority-evacuation (Doc 574) reads as the boundary case where the baseline stops binding entirely.

**Form VI — Pulverization (Doc 445), forward and backward.** Schedule estimation is forward-pulverization (premortem against future schedule risk); earned-value analysis is backward-pulverization (testing actuals against the planned curve). The paired pulverization with two anchors $T = \langle T_I, T_E \rangle$ reads: $T_I$ is the planned schedule (intent), $T_E$ is the actual progress (evidence). Earned value is the operationalized two-anchor pulverization at engagement scale.

**Form V — Hypostatic Boundary (Doc 372).** Schedule management describes work-flow temporal structure, not ontological commitments about the work itself. A schedule says when tasks happen and what depends on what; it does not claim tasks are reducible to their schedule slot.
