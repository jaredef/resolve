## II. Source Read

Definition: a life cycle model is "a framework of processes and activities concerned with the life cycle which can be organized into stages" (ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-1:2024). Four core principles: (1) systems progress through defined stages during their existence, (2) supporting systems must be available to achieve each stage's outcomes, (3) quality characteristics (producibility, usability, supportability, disposability) should be specified at appropriate stages, (4) stages begin and end based on specific criteria or external events. Model characteristics: some permit "iteration and concurrency of stages"; a system can occupy multiple lifecycle stages simultaneously. Common model types referenced: waterfall, incremental, evolutionary; some referenced as "project models, not life cycle models." DevOps reference, verbatim: "One alternative model depicts lifecycle stages in an infinity loop where there is no distinction between development and support phases, assuming minimal retirement considerations." Life-cycle processes are activities integrated into models, often applied "concurrently, iteratively, and recursively" across lifecycle stages.
