## II. Source Read

Quality is defined dually: "the characteristics of a product or service that bear on its ability to satisfy stated or implied needs" and "a product or service free of deficiencies." Juran frames quality as "fitness for use"; Crosby as "conformance to requirements." The SEBoK glossary entry (ISO/IEC/IEEE 2010) gives "ability of a product, service, system, component, or process to meet customer or user needs, expectations, or requirements." The page's organizing principle: "quality cannot be achieved if it cannot be measured." Five strategies named: acceptance sampling (statistical lot inspection), statistical process control (SPC, control charts), design for quality (process robustness against variation), Six Sigma (DMAIC / DMADV), and standards (ISO 9000, Baldrige). Quality attributes vary by deliverable type (products carry physical-tolerance characteristics; services carry availability, reliability, responsiveness, safety, usability; enterprises carry adaptability, maintainability, efficiency). Position: Part 3 SE and Management, Technical Management Processes.

## III. Structural Read

**Form D (co-production at sub-rungs, Doc 573).** QA is canonical co-production at the conformance rung. The substrate-side party (the producing organization) supplies process discipline and measurement records; the keeper-side party (the auditor, the standard, the customer's acceptance regime) supplies the threshold-of-acceptability against which records are read. Neither party alone produces the conformance verdict. The dual definition (Juran's fitness for use, Crosby's conformance to requirements) is the two-anchor pulverization (Doc 445 Refinement A) at the QA rung: $T_I$ as fitness-for-use anchor, $T_E$ as conformance-to-spec anchor. Cluster D membership extends.

**Form E (institutional ground, Doc 571).** ISO 9000, the Baldrige Criteria, and the SEI quality regimes are institutional ground. Section X.5 organization-vs-enterprise reads sharply here: ISO 9000 is the formal-authority component (organization-rung); the accumulated industrial QA tradition (statistical methods, Shewhart's SPC origins, Deming-Juran-Crosby school evolution) is the working-tradition component (enterprise-rung). Cluster E membership extends.

**Form C (architectural school, Doc 538).** SPC, Six Sigma, and acceptance sampling are three distinct schools within the QA practice population. The schools share substrate (measurable process variation) but differ in vocabulary (Shewhart limits vs. DMAIC vs. AQL) and methodology. The SEBoK page's neutral enumeration of all three is school-coexistence at the QA rung; school-borrowing is observable (Six Sigma absorbed SPC's control charts).

**Form A (universal-sibling lattice, Doc 572 Appendix D).** The five strategies (acceptance sampling, SPC, design for quality, Six Sigma, standards) form a candidate universal-sibling set at the QA rung: each binds every QA engagement aspect-wise (a single program runs SPC AND uses standards AND tailors design-for-quality), the discriminator is aspect, not rung-of-application. The case is weaker than the canonical Cluster A members because the five are not strictly co-present in every engagement; treat as candidate, not confirmed instance.

**Form K (virtue constraints, Doc 314).** "Quality cannot be achieved if it cannot be measured" brushes V3 (truth-telling) at the engineering-decision layer. Honest measurement is the precondition for QA's coherence; falsified records collapse the discipline. The SEBoK voice keeps this implicit; the corpus reads V3 as the constitutive virtue of QA practice.
