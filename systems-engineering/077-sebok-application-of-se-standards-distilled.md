## II. Source Read

Standards "provide sets of process, project, and product requirements and associated guidance that organizations use to influence the definition of their organization and project specific processes." Primary standards: ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023, ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-2:2024, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14000:2015, INCOSE SE Handbook 5th Edition. Voluntary nature: "A standard does not in itself impose any obligation upon anyone to follow it. However, such an obligation may be imposed, for example, by legislation or by a contract." Verb forms: shall (strict), should (recommended), can (permissible, preferred over may). Three conformance approaches: full conformance, tailored conformance, compliance (contractually mandated). Standards "are created by bringing together the experience and expertise of relevant stakeholders, such as the producers, sellers, buyers, users, and regulators." Position: Part 3 SE and Management > Systems Engineering Standards knowledge area.

## III. Structural Read

**Cluster E — Institutional ground (Doc 571), at meta-level.** This page is the SEBoK article most directly about institutional ground. It formalizes the relationship between standards (the codified institutional product) and organizational practice (the working tradition that adopts, tailors, or complies). Section X.5 organization-vs-enterprise applies sharply: the standard lives at the organization-component (formal authority through ISO/IEEE/INCOSE governance); the adopting practice lives at the enterprise-component (the accumulated working tradition within the adopting organization). The page articulates the bridge between the two: tailoring is the discipline of moving content across the bridge.

**Cluster A — Universal-sibling lattice (Doc 572 Appendix D).** The three conformance approaches (full / tailored / compliance) bind every standards-adopting engagement universally; the discriminator is conformance-mode, not whether conformance applies. Ninth Appendix D instance.

**Form III — Substrate-and-Keeper Composition (Doc 510), with verb-form discipline.** "Shall / should / can" is canonical keeper-side language-engineering: the verb encodes the authority gradient. Shall = no keeper-side discretion (substrate must comply); should = keeper-side discretion under recommended discipline; can = keeper-side discretion under permitted discipline. Doc 510's apparatus reads the verb-form taxonomy as a calibrated rung-2 keeper supply embedded in the standard's language. The apparatus may benefit from a worked example: standards verb-forms as authority-gradient encoding.

**Cluster K — Virtue constraints (Doc 314), in the consensus-stakeholder framing.** "Standards are created by bringing together the experience and expertise of relevant stakeholders" — producers, sellers, buyers, users, regulators. The consensus-as-warrant framing brushes V3 Truth Over Plausibility: consensus is plausible-by-process but not necessarily true-by-fact. SEBoK's voice keeps the framing operational (consensus is the legitimacy mechanism), not virtue-claim. Doc 372 hypostatic boundary holds.

**Cluster C — Architectural school (Doc 538), with the school's institutional self-articulation.** Standards are the school's formal self-articulation: vocabulary fixed, methodology codified, institutional memory transmissible by document. The page is the SE school articulating its own institutional-ground discipline. Compare Doc 538 Appendix B.5 on school-borrowing: tailoring is structurally bounded school-borrowing, where the adopting school imports the standard's content and modifies it under documented discipline.

**Form IV — Pin-Art Model (Doc 270).** The "tailoring process" identifying which requirements apply to specific context is canonical pin-art: the standard is the substrate whose flow is selectively constrained by the tailoring keeper to produce the organization-specific process.
