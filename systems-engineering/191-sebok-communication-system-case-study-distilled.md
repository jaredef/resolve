## II. Source Read

Part 7 — Systems Engineering Implementation Examples. NextGen: FAA modernization 2004-2025 transforming U.S. air transportation "from a fragmented ground-based navigation system to a net-centric satellite-based navigation" infrastructure. Communication-system-specific content: voice-based communications dominate the fragmented five-layer architecture, with limited real-time weather integration, suboptimal aircraft routing, and capacity constraints. NextGen replaces voice with "digital communication" while implementing net-centric information systems; eight core capabilities include network-enabled information access, performance-based operations, weather-assimilated decision-making. Key tension: seamless information-sharing requirements vs "the current culture of limited information sharing." Five lessons learned: (1) enterprise transformation requires parallel organizational and cultural evolution beyond FAA's direct control, (2) mid-term milestones allow assumption-examination, (3) critical-mass adoption depends on stakeholder incentives (2012 Modernization Act provided financial incentives), (4) traditional V&V methods inadequate for SoS complexity, (5) legacy-system integration persistent challenge. VCF: $170M FBI modernization 2000-2005 abandoned; 9/11 Commission datum: "The FBI's information systems were woefully inadequate. The FBI lacked the ability to know what it knew." Failure causes: flash-cut-over plan with no backup; bypassed formal requirements development; ~400 change requests Dec 2002-Dec 2003; five CIOs in four years; 8 parallel teams producing 700,000+ lines of code on incomplete requirements spanning 800 pages. IBS: Russian IT firm 2001-present transformation; capability-based agile transformation; "formal regulation to fill in engineering knowledge accumulation templates did not work" — informal common folders proved more practical.
