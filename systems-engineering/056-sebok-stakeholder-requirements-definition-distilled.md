## II. Source Read

The legacy ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2008 process taxonomy named two upstream-of-system-requirements processes: *Stakeholder Requirements Definition* and *Requirements Analysis*. Subsequent INCOSE work (GtNR v1, NRM v1.1) and ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 reframed the upstream rung as *Stakeholder Needs and Requirements Definition*, splitting it into needs-elicitation (SE-030) and the formalization of those needs into a stakeholder-side requirements set. SEBoK's current editorial state carries the reframing: the *System Requirements* page redirects, the formalization rung lives partly in *Stakeholder Needs Definition* and partly in *System Requirements Definition*. The latter distinguishes "stakeholder requirements" (external, user-focused, e.g., "The user needs the coffee maker to stop heating water once the user-selected temperature has been achieved") from "system requirements" (internal, technical, e.g., "The Coffee Maker shall stop the heating function once the selected Brew Temperature has been achieved"), naming the transformation as functional analysis, interface analysis, data-flow analysis. INCOSE NRM 2022 carries the formal stakeholder-requirements artifact.

## III. Structural Read

**Form XI — Co-Production at Sub-Rungs (Doc 573), at the formalization sub-rung.** Stakeholder requirements are the keeper-side rung-2 formalization of the co-produced needs (SE-030). The activity is structurally distinct from stakeholder needs definition: needs are jointly held in stakeholder vocabulary, stakeholder requirements rephrase the needs in a form binding for downstream system-requirements work without yet crossing into system-internal language. Doc 573's three patterns apply at the rephrasing rung: keeper-proposes (engineer drafts, stakeholder confirms), substrate-proposes (stakeholder writes the binding statement, engineer structures), on-the-fly (joint workshop drafting).

**Form X — Institutional Ground (Doc 571), with Section X.5 organization-vs-enterprise sub-form.** The page's non-existence is a Cluster E observation in itself. The institutional carrier (the SEBoK editorial board, INCOSE) has migrated the formalization rung from a standalone article into surrounding articles. The §X.5 distinction reads: the organization-component (15288 / 29148 / NRM 2022) still names the rung; the enterprise-component (the working tradition) integrates it into Stakeholder Needs Definition and System Requirements Definition, leaving the standalone page as red link. This is a stable rather than evacuated state: the rung is named in the surrounding pages and in the standards.

**Form III (extension) — Lattice Extension of the Ladder (Doc 572).** Stakeholder requirements sit at a distinct rung in the requirements ladder: substrate-supplied need (rung-1) → integrated stakeholder needs (rung-2 keeper supply, SE-030) → stakeholder requirements (formalized rung-2 packaging) → system requirements (rung-3 transformation, SE-024). The lattice is denser than the SEBoK editorial state shows.

**Form III — Substrate-and-Keeper Composition (Doc 510).** Single-keeper case: the SE keeper authoring the stakeholder-requirements artifact against the multi-stakeholder substrate. The Doc 604 multi-keeper composition is upstream (at needs definition); the formalization rung is substrate-and-keeper at engagement scope.

**Form IV — Pin-Art Model (Doc 270).** Each stakeholder-requirement statement is a pin into which the substrate's articulated need is pressed; the keeper supplies the pin-set (the requirements template, the binding-statement form). The shape that emerges is the stakeholder-requirements baseline.
