## III. Structural Read

**Cluster E — Institutional ground (Doc 571), canonical knowledge-area instance.** Where Cluster E members surfaced through individual Part 6 pages (HSI SE-038, Roles and Competencies SE-033, etc.), this knowledge area is Cluster E surveyed directly. Every standard the sub-articles enumerate (ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288, 15289, 24748, 29148, 42010, INCOSE SEH, MIL-STD-499, etc., per the broader Part 3 references) is one institutional ground's formal codification. Doc 571 §X.5 binds: each standard is an organization-component artifact (formal authority, balloted text); the practice tradition each standard documents is the enterprise-component (the accumulated working tradition the standard tries to capture).

**Cluster B — Multi-keeper composition (Doc 510 / Doc 604), at the standards-alignment rung.** The "Alignment and Comparison" sub-article is itself a Cluster B instance. Multiple standards bodies (ISO, IEC, IEEE, INCOSE, NDIA, government agencies) each co-keep their own standards; alignment work is keeper-side reconciliation across grounds. The reconciliation rung is the joint working group (e.g., the ISO/IEC JTC1/SC7 + IEEE collaboration that produces the joint 15288 standards). Subordination-by-domain partially applies (each body has its terrain); negotiation-by-priority is the operative composition rule for alignment work.

**Cluster C — Architectural school (Doc 538).** Standards are the school's frozen articulation. Every named standard is the SE school's codification at a moment, the practice tradition pressed into formal text. The page's framing of "suitable application" (the fourth sub-article) is the school's own discipline of when to invoke which codification — the school knowing how to wear its own standards.

**The page is structurally light on its own content** — most depth lives in the four sub-articles. The Cluster E instance is robust; Cluster B's alignment-work case is the structurally novel reading the corpus surfaces.
