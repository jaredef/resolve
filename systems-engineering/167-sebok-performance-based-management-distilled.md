## II. Source Read

The target page does not exist. *Measurement* is the canonical carrier for performance-based management content within SEBoK. Measurement and analysis are "fundamental elements of systems engineering and technical management," providing information about products, services, and processes to support effective management and objective quality evaluation. The four-activity measurement process: Establish and Sustain Commitment (resources, training, management buy-in), Plan Measurement (define measures aligned with decision-makers' information needs), Perform Measurement (collect, prepare, analyze, present), Evaluate Measurement (assess relevance to business goals). Three frameworks: PSM (Practical Software and Systems Measurement, information categories and measurable concepts), GQM (Goal Question Metric), ISO/IEC/IEEE 15939 (international standard). Three technical-measure types: MOEs (Measures of Effectiveness — how well the system meets mission needs), MOPs (Measures of Performance — how well system components function), TPMs (Technical Performance Measures — track specific attributes throughout development). Leading indicators "provide visibility into expected project performance and potential future states." Four critical pitfalls: golden measures, single-pass, data-without-information-need, inappropriate-use. The *Systems Engineering and Project Management* knowledge area indexes performance-management-related topics (PMBOK, PM-SE relationship) but does not develop performance-based management as a standalone discipline.

**Cluster K (virtue constraints, Doc 314), V3-as-procedure-binding at the four-activity process and the inappropriate-use pitfall.** The four-activity procedure exists because measurement-by-discretion exhibits rankism (the measurer ranks their preferred measure above the decision-maker's information need) and complacency (measurement-without-evaluation lets stale measures persist). The inappropriate-use pitfall ("not evaluating individual performance") is V3 explicitly: measurement that conflates capability evaluation with personal evaluation rankism-fails the practitioner. Two V3 sub-modes: procedure-binding and use-binding. Cluster K density grows.
