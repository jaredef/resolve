## II. Source Read

The discipline is dispersed. *System Requirements Definition* supplies the specification-side activities (translate stakeholder requirements into system requirements, verify for completeness/consistency, allocate to architecture). *Requirements Management* (SE-125) supplies the lifecycle-management activities (baseline, change-control, traceability). *Requirements Flow-down and Allocation* (SE-158) supplies the decomposition discipline (parent-to-child requirement allocation across architectural decomposition). *Stakeholder Requirements Traceability* (SE-122) supplies the bidirectional trace discipline. The verbatim distinctions from the surrounding pages: requirements are typed (functional, performance, interface, environmental, design-constraint, regulatory, "-ility") and criticality-classified (mission-critical, safety-critical, mission-essential, mission-support, non-essential); specification is the act of writing atomic shall-statements with verification methods named at write-time; analysis is the act of testing the specification for completeness, consistency, feasibility, and verifiability before baseline. Standards: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288, INCOSE GtWR (Guide to Writing Requirements).
