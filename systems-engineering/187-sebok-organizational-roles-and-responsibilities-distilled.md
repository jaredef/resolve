## II. Source Read

Lead authors Richard Beasley, Art Pyster, Hillary Sillitto. Part 5 > Enabling Systems Engineering > Enabling Businesses and Enterprises. Verbatim: businesses and enterprises are entities with "sufficiently strong central authority and motivation to take steps to enable SE"; infostructure is the "information infrastructure supporting the system lifecycle, including process libraries, modeling tools, shared workspaces, and IT facilities." Five organizational structures: (1) project organization (projects manage all resources/staffing), (2) functional organization (delegate to specialized functional groups), (3) matrix organization (SE professionals with home-base between projects), (4) integrated organization (SE tasks performed without specific functional allegiance), (5) product-centered organization (structure mirrors system breakdown structure with integrated product teams). Role-typologies: Sheard (1996) twelve SE roles; Sheard (2000) discovery-phase (high uncertainty) vs program-phase (deterministic); Kasser et al. (2009) five SE types at increasing levels of abstraction, ambiguity, scope, and innovation; Sillitto (2011a) various SE roles within engineering and business landscapes. Adjacent: SE-033 *Roles and Competencies*, SE-138 *Organizational Structures and Roles*, SE-157 *Competency Assessment*.

**Cluster C architectural school (Doc 538), at the structure-choice rung.** Each of the five organizational structures carries a school's stance on SE authority: project-organization-school treats SE as project-resourced; functional-school treats SE as a discipline that lends specialists; matrix-school is the school of dual-allegiance; integrated-school is post-allegiance; product-centered-school binds SE to product-line lifecycle. Five schools at one decision-rung. Cluster C density grows; the school-pairing-types (engineering-engineering at SE-026, engineering-engagement at SE-037, engineering-non-engineering at SE-117) gain a fourth pairing: school-vs-school-within-discipline (where the schools all claim SE authority but locate it differently). Worth flagging.

**Cluster K V3-as-procedure-binding, role-formalization mode.** Roles and responsibilities exist as procedural binding because the discipline cannot rely on virtue (clarity-of-authority, hand-off-discipline) alone in a multi-keeper organization. The Beasley/Sheard/Kasser typologies are V3-shaped: name the role explicitly so the responsibility cannot be silently absorbed or rankism-distorted. V3 sub-mode: role-binding, candidate eighth sub-mode after gate-binding (SE-185) and evidential-binding (SE-186).

**Cluster H hypostatic-boundary, role-vs-person.** The role-typology discipline preserves the role-vs-person distinction: Sheard's twelve roles are roles, not people; one person can hold multiple roles, one role can be held by multiple people. The discipline names the functional surface (what the role does) without claiming the person IS the role. Hypostatic-boundary at the role rung.
