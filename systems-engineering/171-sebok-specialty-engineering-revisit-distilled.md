## II. Source Read

Definition reaffirmed: specialty engineering is "the collective engineering of all quality attributes of a system." Updated 12-topic enumeration: Framework for Viewing Quality Attributes from Loss Lens; Human Systems Integration; Manufacturability and Producibility; System Adaptability; System Affordability; System Hardware Assurance; System Reliability, Availability, and Maintainability; System Resilience; Resilience Modeling; System Resistance to Electromagnetic Interference; System Safety; System Security. Three integration filters, verbatim: First filter — "Conceptual analysis that leverages traditional design consideration (structural, electronics, aerodynamics, mechanical, thermodynamics, etc.)"; Second filter — "specialty disciplines including safety, affordability, quality assurance, human factors, and reliability assessments"; Third filter — "facility design, equipment, procedures, software, and personnel requirements." Trade-off claim: "Making a system more secure may make it harder to use but also safer." Scales of engagement: "Systems engineers address quality attributes through specification, measurement definition, conflict resolution, and verification activities integrated throughout development lifecycles."

The lattice has 11 quality-attribute siblings + 1 meta-frame sibling. New Cluster A sub-form candidate: *meta-axis* — one sibling is a viewing-frame for the others, structurally one rung up but enumerated as a sibling. Distinct from existing universal-sibling, two-axis, ordinal-axis, multi-rung, scale-axis, and definitional-triplet sub-forms. Awaits second instance for formalization.

**Cluster B trade-off claim ("more secure may make it harder to use but also safer").** Three-keeper trade-off (security ⇄ usability ⇄ safety) is a sharper formulation than SE-065's two-keeper reading (security ⇄ usability). The trade-off is non-monotonic: increasing security may decrease usability AND increase safety simultaneously. New refinement candidate: *non-monotonic three-keeper trade-off* — Cluster B sub-form where one keeper-shift produces opposite-signed effects on two other keepers. Structurally distinct from negotiation-by-priority (where shifts trade off pairwise in same direction).

**Cluster F (longitudinal-pulverization, SE-114) at lifecycle-integration rung.** "Verification activities integrated throughout development lifecycles" reads as Cluster F at the verification-throughout rung. **Cluster K (virtue constraints) at conflict-resolution rung.** "Conflict resolution" as one of the four scales of engagement reads as V3-as-procedure-binding at the trade-off rung: the procedure binds because virtue is structurally insufficient when keepers' outputs conflict.
